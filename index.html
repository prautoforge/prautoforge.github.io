Built for the work, not for a demo.

Each module produces something you can hand to an auditor, a board, an insurer or a customer. Every file stays on your computer, in formats you own: Word, Excel and HTML.

01 Compliance readiness

Assess once. See every framework.

Answer 52 safeguard questions: in place, partly, planned, not in place or not applicable. Readiness for every framework you track is calculated requirement by requirement, with the gaps that matter most listed first.

  • SOC 2, ISO 27001:2022 Annex A, NIST CSF 2.0, CIS v8.1, HIPAA, PCI DSS 4.0, NIS2, GDPR, CMMC Level 1, NIST 800-171, Cyber Essentials, Essential Eight and cyber insurance
  • AI governance: ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF
  • An ISO 27001 Statement of Applicability, ready for your auditor
  • Your approved questionnaire answers count as evidence
How readiness works
02 Incident response

Every deadline, counting down.

Record an incident and the app lists who must be told and by when: your cyber insurer, the data protection authority under GDPR, NIS2 and DORA authorities, the SEC, HHS, NY DFS, card brands, US states and your customers. Run the response from a playbook for the kind of incident you have.

  • Playbooks for ransomware, business email compromise, account takeover, data exposure and five more
  • A timeline, lessons learned and an incident report for leadership, insurer and counsel
Incident response in the guide
03 Third-party risk

Know which vendors can hurt you.

Each vendor is tiered Critical, High, Moderate or Low from the data it holds, the access it has, how much you depend on it and how it connects. The tier sets the review schedule and the size of the questionnaire you send. Score the answers when they come back.

  • Vendor security questionnaires in Excel, sized to the tier
  • Scored answers with red flags and residual risk
  • Reviews on your compliance calendar
Vendor risk in the guide
04 Risk register and policies

Risks with owners. Policies with signatures.

Keep every risk with its likelihood, impact, treatment, owner and due date, on a heat map leadership can read. Risks are suggested from your safeguard gaps. Seventeen security policies are written from your company profile, with framework references, ready to adapt and approve.

  • Policies as Word, HTML and Markdown, with approval and yearly review dates
  • Which staff signed which version, and who completed security training
  • A posture report for leadership or the board, printable to PDF
Risks and policies in the guide
05 Security questionnaires

Answers you can stand behind.

Fill the buyer's own spreadsheet from answers your team approved. Questions without evidence, legal questions and suspicious cells are set aside for a person. The claim guard checks every edit and the final file before it is sent, so nothing claims more than you can prove.

  • Optional AI drafting with a model on your computer or your own cloud key
  • A customer trust pack from the statements you marked shareable
The questionnaire workflow
06 Evidence and automated checks

Evidence ready before the auditor asks.

Attach screenshots, exports and reports to each safeguard; each file is stored with its SHA-256 checksum and an expiry date if it has one. Checks run every week and file their own results: your domains' email and web security, your GitHub settings, and the MFA and device exports from your identity provider and device management.

  • Domain grades A to F: SPF, DKIM, DMARC, MTA-STS, DNSSEC, certificates, TLS and security headers
  • One audit package zip with every document and evidence file, and an index of checksums
Evidence in the guide
07 Vulnerabilities and access

Every finding has a date. Every account has an owner.

Import scans from Nessus, Qualys, Microsoft Defender or OpenVAS. Each finding gets a fix-by date from its severity, with 7 days for anything on CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list, and findings that disappear from the next scan are closed. Review who has access to each system from its user export: administrators, accounts without MFA, unused accounts and people who left are flagged for a decision.

  • Accepted risks need a written reason, kept for the auditor
  • The signed-off access review is saved as evidence
Vulnerabilities in the guide
08 Privacy, AI and your team

Records of processing, an AI inventory, a shared program.

Keep records of processing with DPIA screening and an inventory of the AI tools your company uses or builds. Put the program in a folder your team shares and everyone works on the same one, with locked saves and an activity log that names who changed what.

  • A trust center website with your safeguards, subprocessors, policies and security.txt, to host anywhere
  • My work: each person sees the safeguards, risks, reviews and findings assigned to them
