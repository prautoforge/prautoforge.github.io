SIG Lite vs CAIQ: what small software vendors should know

If you sell software to larger companies, you will eventually receive a SIG Lite or a CAIQ, or a custom questionnaire that borrows from both. They cover similar ground, but they come from different organisations and are used in different ways.

Who publishes them

SIG (Standardized Information Gathering) questionnaires are published by Shared Assessments, a membership organisation, and are licensed content. SIG Lite is the shorter version, commonly used for an initial or lower-risk vendor review. The full SIG Core is much longer.

CAIQ (Consensus Assessments Initiative Questionnaire) is published by the Cloud Security Alliance and is free to download. It maps to the CSA Cloud Controls Matrix. Version 4 contains 261 questions according to CSA's own description, most answered yes or no with room for comments.

How they differ in practice

Question counts change between versions, so check the edition your buyer sent rather than relying on a number from an article.

Which one should you prepare first?

Prepare whichever your buyers actually send. If you sell a cloud product and nobody has asked yet, a completed CAIQ is a reasonable starting point: it is free, widely recognised, and some vendors publish theirs so buyers can review it without a custom questionnaire.

Answer once, reuse everywhere

The controls behind both questionnaires overlap heavily: access control, encryption, logging, vulnerability management, incident response, business continuity, vendor management and personnel security. Write one approved answer per control in an answer library, with the evidence behind it, and you can answer either questionnaire, and most custom ones, from the same source.

A note on copyright

Do not redistribute SIG content you do not have a license for. Answer the copy your customer sends you, and keep your library in your own words.
