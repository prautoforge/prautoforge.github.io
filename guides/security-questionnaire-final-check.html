Free checklist

Check the security questionnaire you actually send

An answer can be accurate in your library and wrong in the final spreadsheet. Someone shortens a sentence, removes a qualification or changes a commitment. Review the exported file after those edits.

Seven checks before sending

  1. Evidence: each factual claim points to an approved statement or a source the owner has reviewed. A polished answer is not evidence.
  2. Scope: check the product, environment and region. A control used for one service may not cover another.
  3. Commitments: flag response times, recovery targets, availability promises and words such as “always.” Ask the responsible owner to confirm them.
  4. Certifications: distinguish having a policy, following a framework and holding a certification. Do not substitute one for another.
  5. Dates: make sure the approved statement is still current. Recheck statements after control or policy changes.
  6. Gaps: leave unsupported answers for an owner rather than filling them with plausible language. Keep an explicit list of open questions.
  7. Final file: review the copy going to the buyer, including edits made outside your review tool. Keep that version with the evidence references.

A small edit with a different meaning

Fictional example: an approved statement says “Backups run daily for the production database.” An edit says “All data is backed up continuously.” Both the scope and the frequency changed. The second answer needs its own evidence; the first statement does not support it.

Use the checklist with your team

Assign an owner to each open item. Separate questions needing evidence from ones needing legal review. Run the checks again after edits, and agree who can approve the final copy.
